The Engineering Problem Atmospheric turbulence introduces stochastic refractive index fluctuations that distort coherent laser wavefronts. In free-space optical communications, this causes severe speckle, phase distortion, and beam wander that degrade signal reception. SHYENA applies machine learning and adaptive optics to estimate the perturbed wavefront from sensor slopes and command corrective mirror deformation to restore concentrated optical focus.
Stage 01
Atmospheric Disturbance
Kolmogorov turbulence introduces spatial refractive index eddies along the propagation path.
Stage 02
Wavefront Distortion
Optical path differences create severe phase aberrations across the receiving pupil.
Stage 03
Wavefront Sensing
A 4×4 Shack-Hartmann lenslet array measures local spatial phase gradients (32 slope features).
Stage 04
AI Estimation
Regularized Ridge regression solves normal equations to predict low-order Zernike modal weights.
Stage 05
Adaptive Correction
Deformable mirror applies conjugate phase subtraction, restoring an intact Airy disk at the detector.

THEORY & IMPLEMENTATION

Computational Methods

An end-to-end mathematical narrative of how coherent optical wavefronts are modeled, sensed, estimated, and reconstructed.

Step 1: Atmospheric Modeling Implemented
Kolmogorov Turbulence

Simulates optical path length fluctuations using the modified von Kármán power spectral density. Computes spatial phase screens via inverse 2D Fast Fourier Transform.

$$\Phi(f) = 0.023\, r_0^{-5/3} (f^2 + f_0^2)^{-11/6}$$
Step 2: Optical Propagation Implemented
Fraunhofer Diffraction

Propagates the complex pupil field $E = A \cdot \exp(i\varphi)$ into far-field intensity. Accurately predicts speckle patterns and Strehl ratio degradation.

$$I(\mathbf{u}) = \left| \mathcal{F}\left\{ A(r) e^{i\varphi(r)} \right\} \right|^2$$
Step 3: Wavefront Sensing Implemented
Shack-Hartmann Subapertures

Divides pupil into $4 \times 4$ lenslets. Evaluates average spatial gradients ($s_x, s_y$) per subaperture, producing 32 slope features per scenario.

$$s_x = \frac{1}{A}\iint \frac{\partial \varphi}{\partial x} \, dA + \eta$$
Step 4: AI Estimation Implemented
Ridge Regularization

Solves closed-form normal equations with an $L_2$ penalty to map slope features to 15 Zernike aberration modes, preventing collinear overfitting.

$$\hat{\beta} = (X^T X + \alpha I)^{-1} X^T y$$
Step 5: Wavefront Recovery Implemented
Zernike Modal Synthesis

Reconstructs the continuous estimated phase screen from predicted orthogonal polynomial coefficients and calculates residual error.

$$\hat{\varphi}(r, \theta) = \sum_{j=1}^{15} \hat{a}_j Z_j(r, \theta)$$
Step 6: Closed-Loop Control Planned / Conceptual
Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Proposed Markov decision process: agent receives sensor slopes, commands deformable mirror actuator voltages, and optimizes Strehl ratio reward.

$$R_t = \text{Strehl}_t - \lambda \|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2$$

Test RMSE vs Number of Realizations

Evaluates sample efficiency and model capacity plateau.

Regularization Prevents Overfitting

Adjacent Shack-Hartmann subapertures measure spatially correlated wavefront gradients. Ridge regression's $L_2$ penalty dampens collinear weight blowups, achieving ~11.4% lower test RMSE.

Low-Order Modes Dominate

Tip, tilt, and defocus (Zernike modes 2, 3, 4) contain over 70% of turbulent phase energy. Linear models reconstruct these effectively, while higher-order spherical modes require non-linear networks.

Sample Capacity Plateau

Increasing realizations beyond 100 samples yields diminishing returns. Linear models hit a mathematical capacity ceiling, highlighting the future role of physics-informed neural networks.

SCIENTIFIC INTEGRITY

Research Limitations

Honest reflection on study boundaries as cataloged in Section 9 of the project report (Pages 32–33).

1
Simulation Model Constraints
Kolmogorov and von Kármán spectral models assume isotropic, stationary turbulence and do not fully capture intermittent atmospheric gusts or localized micro-weather.
2
Absence of Real-Time Sensor Telemetry
The study evaluated synthetic phase screens without hardware sensor feedback, omitting CCD dark current, readout noise, photon shot noise, and physical lenslet misalignment.
3
Reinforcement Learning Scaling & Latency
RL agents require thousands of episodes to converge. In physical deployment, microsecond control loop deadlines risk policy instability and latency-induced phase lag.
4
Linear Regression Capacity Limits
Linear and Ridge estimators cannot learn complex phase wrapping or nonlinear diffraction dynamics present during deep optical scintillation.
5
Hybrid Model Coordination
Combining regression-based feedforward estimation with RL feedback controllers creates coupling challenges that can produce antagonistic actuator commands if uncalibrated.
6
Incomplete Communication-Level Evaluation
Evaluation focuses on optical phase and Strehl metrics; full communications parameters such as bit-error rate (BER), eye diagrams, and constellation SNR remain unmeasured.
7
SWaP Constraints for Aerospace Deployment
Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) budgets for satellite and drone payloads require dedicated low-power FPGA or edge-GPU acceleration not modeled here.
8
Fault Tolerance & Failsafe Layers
Current software lacks anomaly detection and watchdog mechanisms to gracefully recover when extreme turbulence blinds the sensor or saturates mirror stroke.
